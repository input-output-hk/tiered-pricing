# [Experimentation](https://github.com/input-output-hk/tiered-pricing/issues/54)

This fortnight closed the remaining gaps between what the simulator measured and what a ledger rule can enforce, then refreshed the headline evidence at the full adopted configuration.

**Headroom bound.** The specified admission and producer headroom is the exact largest upward controller step, which is 2.08% on the standard lane at target 0.75. The historical experiment code floored that bound at 1/D, which is 6.25%. A paired smoke (seeds 430-439, 2,000 slots, all five headline loads) compared the two bounds: every reported scalar was bit-identical in all ten seed pairs on every load, so the experimental evidence applies unchanged to the rule as specified. This is a bounded check, conditional on the sender model.

**Announcement semantics and the standard window.** A code review found two places where the simulator diverged from an enforceable rule: zero-width EB-announcement entries occupied standard-window positions, which shortened the effective window under load, and the age-escape count reset at every announcement rather than at certificate inclusion. Announcement counting cannot become a ledger rule, because an announcement is a cost-free producer claim, so counting it would give every producer a free lever on the standard quote. Four experiments selected and confirmed the replacement:

- A smoke (seeds 440-449, the five headline loads plus the 0.1 tx/slot trickle) showed a naive 20-processed-block window loses launch-day value against the historical behaviour, so the length had to be chosen deliberately.
- A length screen (seeds 0-99, lengths 10, 13, 15, and 20) found launch-day retained value monotone in the length. Ten blocks won: +2.32 G lovelace over historical (95% CI [+1.98, +2.66], better in 94 of 100 seeds), with every other load bit-identical or within a few thousandths of a percentage point. Ten blocks is roughly the effective depth the historical window ran at under load.
- A confirmation on disjoint seeds 700-799 tested the full adopted configuration, the 10-block window plus the certification-time reset, against pre-registered criteria: match historical at the contended loads, stay within 1 percentage point of it at trickle, and keep the margin over flat fee under contention. Every criterion passed. Superiority over historical was a declared secondary and was observed anyway (launch day +1.91 G, higher in 90 of 100 seed pairs). The certification-time reset is an improvement in its own right: +5.9 percentage points of trickle standard retained value over the announcement-reset policy.
- A final smoke (seeds 450-459) bounded the one remaining divergence, the simulator's announcement-time qualification check. The contended loads were bit-identical in every seed, and at trickle every interval spans zero, with the standard-lane point estimates favouring the specified rule, so the simulated evidence is conservative for the rule as written.

**Headline refresh at the adopted configuration.** The five headline comparisons against flat fee were rerun at the full adopted configuration (0.75 standard target, 10-block window, certification-time reset): urgent-class retained value +3.69 percentage points at mid load, +6.54 under severe congestion, and +3.68 under EB-capacity stress, overall retained value +13.11 points under launch day (each in ten of ten seeds), and the low-load urgent-class comparison null. The EB-stress figure is smaller than the historical +8.57 because the 0.75 standard target admits about 29% more urgent-class demand; the class retains more absolute value while its average falls.

**Thousand-seed replication regenerated.** The 1,000-seed low and severe-congestion replication was regenerated at the adopted configuration, and the CIP now quotes the regenerated record. Under severe congestion, urgent-class retained value improves by +6.63 percentage points (95% CI [+6.53, +6.72], all 1,000 seeds better) and urgent-class mean latency falls from 2.97 to 2.58 blocks. The predictability figures for the CPS also come from this record: the urgent-class 95th-percentile wait falls from 5.74 to 5.03 blocks, and the distance between its median and its 95th percentile narrows in the block measure at both replicated loads. The low-load cost is unchanged in kind: overall retained value sits 0.41 points below flat fee while transactions that stay standard wait 2.99 blocks against 1.78.

Simulator work in support: processed-block window semantics, the certification-time reset, a flag implementing the specified qualification-time rule, and the usual sweep manifests, runners, and pinned comparison scripts.
