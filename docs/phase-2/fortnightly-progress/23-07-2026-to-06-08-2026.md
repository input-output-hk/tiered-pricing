# [Experimentation](https://github.com/input-output-hk/tiered-pricing/issues/54)

This fortnight hardened the evidence behind the recommended mechanism: a thousand-seed replication, two robustness experiments at severe congestion, and a low-load cost attribution.

**Thousand-seed replication at low and severe-congestion load.** The ten-seed headlines bound seed-sampling error loosely. We replicated the flat-fee versus canonical pairing at 1,000 paired seeds (0-999, 2,000 slots, independent streams) under the two extremes of the load axis:

- Severe congestion: the replication confirms and sharpens the headline. Urgent retained value improves by +7.16 percentage points (95% CI [+7.06, +7.26]), with all 1,000 seeds improved. Urgent mean latency falls in every seed. Overall retained value rises by +0.38 percentage points.
- Low load: the parity bound tightens by an order of magnitude (-0.03 ± 0.12 percentage points urgent retained value). Urgent-class users submit about 7.6% more transactions, at unchanged retention and service rate, because a reserved lane exists.
- The larger sample resolved a cost invisible at ten seeds. Overall retained value at low load sits 0.40 ± 0.01 percentage points below flat fee. The standard lane carries the whole cost (mean wait 57.96 slots against 34.25 under flat fee) while it pools transactions into endorser blocks worth their certificate. The urgent lane loses nothing. This prices the trade that the specification accepts: 0.40 points of low-load retention for the +7.16-point improvement under severe congestion.

**Urgent-class entry and composition at severe congestion.** This experiment tested whether demand composition inflates the severe-congestion headline, since dynamic quotes change who submits. A paired ten-seed rerun compared the entry-facing scalars. Under the mechanism the urgent class submits about 7% less value. Yet it retains +235M lovelace more in absolute terms (nine of ten seeds) and loses 678M less (ten of ten). The gain is real service, not a composition effect.

**Lane-choice belief calibration at severe congestion.** The simulated actors hold fixed latency beliefs (50 slots standard, 25 priority), and the realized latencies differ. We replaced the defaults with the realized values and reran the headline pairing. Every directional claim survived. The headline shrinks by 0.60 ± 0.24 percentage points (all ten seeds), so roughly 92% of the effect is robust to the beliefs.

**Low-load attribution at a hundred seeds.** A dedicated paired rerun (seeds 0-99, per-lane value levels preserved) decomposed the 0.40-point low-load cost. Entry effects are negligible. The whole difference arises among submitted transactions, from value that decays during the longer standard-lane wait.

**Admission headroom correction.** The admission rule now requires the posted max fee to cover the applicable lane quotes one worst-case controller step ahead. For an urgent transaction this means both lane quotes, because it can settle at either quote. At the recommended settings that is about 6.25% of headroom.

**Prepared, not yet run:** a default-threshold ablation (fixed thresholds at 1 byte, one-quarter RB, and three-quarter RB) and a loose producer-headroom variant. The evidence-scope note in the CIP records this rerun as pending.

# [Documentation](https://github.com/input-output-hk/tiered-pricing/issues/37)

The CIP advanced in step with the experiments:

- We rewrote the abstract in plain language. It states the one-sixth headline gain for urgent value, the low-load cost on standard transactions, and where the exact figures are.
- New Rationale sections cover the thousand-seed replication, urgent-class entry and composition, and lane-choice belief calibration. The tiered-pricing comparison now sits in its own subsection, "Why not full tiered pricing?".
- An evidence-scope note records which tables predate the fee-cap correction and the stream separation, and which postdate both.
- The validation evidence in the threshold section now uses per-experiment blocks: hypothesis, experiment description, interpretation.
- We generalised the announcement threshold from the byte-only simulator gate to the full resource model: transaction size, reference scripts, and both execution-unit components. Comparisons use integer-only consensus arithmetic.
- The CIP now states the threshold and the age escape as chain-only rules. A validator checks both at certificate inclusion. The escape counter resets on certificate inclusion, not on announcement.
- We acted on review feedback across the document and updated the author list.
