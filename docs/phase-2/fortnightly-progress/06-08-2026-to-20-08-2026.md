# [Experimentation](https://github.com/input-output-hk/tiered-pricing/issues/54)

This fortnight answered the remaining calibration questions with four dedicated experiments, and added a predictability result for the CPS.

**Latency distribution at 1,000 seeds.** CPS-0031 names a reduction in wait-time spread for urgent transactions as an evaluation criterion, and every earlier latency figure was a mean. We extended the thousand-seed record with median and 95th-percentile order statistics. All 22 earlier metric summaries reproduced exactly, per-seed values included. The result, in the block measure: under severe congestion the urgent-class 95th-percentile wait falls from 5.74 to 4.79 blocks. The distance between the median and the 95th percentile falls from 3.30 to 2.79 blocks. At low load that distance falls from 0.96 to 0.54 blocks. The slot-measure spread does not narrow, because the interval between Ranking Blocks is exogenous and no fee rule can remove it.

**Default-threshold ablation.** The prepared ablation ran, and its record is preserved. It compared fixed announcement gates at 1 byte, one-quarter RB, and three-quarter RB with the half-RB default, plus a one-RB quick check at 20 seeds. At low and mid load, higher gates improved urgent retention and latency and delayed standard traffic (three-quarter RB against half: +1.94 and +0.52 percentage points urgent retained value, +0.232 and +0.026 blocks standard latency). The contrasts at the three heavier loads were zero or near zero.

**Standard-lane target screen and confirmation.** A dedicated experiment swept the standard-lane target independently (0.625, 0.75, 0.875, with the urgent target held at 0.5) with a demand-census arm. The screen ran on seeds 0-199 and the confirmation on fresh seeds 200-299. The recommended standard-lane target rose from 0.5 to 0.75. On the confirmation seeds, the 0.75 calibration retained at least as much overall and urgent-class value as 0.5 at every compared load.

**Window-removal experiment.** This experiment asks whether the two controller windows are necessary at all. A pilot on seeds 300-304 selected two effects to confirm, and we recorded hashes of the pilot report and the analysis plan before any confirmation seed ran. The confirmation on fresh seeds 400-409 passed both pre-registered tests in ten of ten seeds. Without the standard window, launch-day throughput falls by 18.7 tx/slot and overall retained value by 121.3 G lovelace. Without the urgent window, retained value shows no meaningful change, but quote movement increases at every load (excess log-coefficient travel 0.532 to 3.487 under severe congestion). Both windows stay, for different reasons: service during surges, and quote stability.

**Non-certificate window contribution.** The standard controller counts a non-certificate Ranking Block as its own capacity with zero usage. This experiment replaced that contribution with alternatives from zero to a third of an empty EB, plus a controller that updates only on certified EBs. The RB-sized arm reproduced the recommended design bit-for-bit at every load, which confirms the comparison path. Every contribution larger than the RB capacity lost launch-day retained value, monotonically up to -119.2 G lovelace at EB/3, and the certificate-only controller lost 54.6 G. Contributions smaller than the RB capacity moved outcomes by less than half a percent. The RB-sized choice stays.

Simulator work in support: sweep manifests, runners, pinned comparison scripts, and preserved provenance records for all four experiments, plus up-front sweep-configuration parsing.

# [Documentation](https://github.com/input-output-hk/tiered-pricing/issues/37)

**The CIP branch merged into main on 19 August** ([PR #83](https://github.com/input-output-hk/tiered-pricing/pull/83)). The merge landed the full phase-2 specification and evidence base on main. Content added during the fortnight:

- **Path to Active.** The acceptance criteria are a checklist of twelve conditions. They include CIP-164 activation, the fee change CIP, upstream specification merges, a peer-reviewed implementation, conformance tests, testnet load tests with declared margins, an independent audit, and wallet support. The implementation plan lists the work items in approximate order.
- **How this CIP addresses CPS-0031.** A new section answers the CPS goals (reduce value destroyed by delay, permissionless access, predictability over raw speed), its constraints (ledger determinism, on-chain record, censorship resistance, linear-Leios compatibility), and each of its open questions.
- **Incentives.** Giorgos added a summary of the incentive analysis. It argues that the design satisfies the three properties from the EIP-1559 analysis: incentive compatibility for myopic block producers, user incentive compatibility, and off-chain-agreement proofness. He also added a comment on MEV.
- **Specification sections.** Polina corrected and completed her mempool and ledger sections during the review.
- **Calibration updates.** The recommended construction table now carries the 0.75 standard-lane target, the window-removal and non-certificate-contribution results, and an extended evidence-scope note that separates pre-correction from post-correction experiments.
- **Review pass.** We resolved the review comments across the document, cleaned the glossary, and renamed `maxBlockSize` to `maxBlockBodySize`.
